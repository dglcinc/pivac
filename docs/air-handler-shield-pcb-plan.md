# Air-handler node shield: PCB plan

**Status:** Plan and floor plan drawn; no board generated yet. · **Owner:** David

The first node, at the master bedroom, is an Arduino UNO R4 WiFi with a hand-wired Olimex PROTO-SHIELD, a DIN-rail 24 VAC to
USB-C buck converter, a Magnecraft 792 relay on a socket and nine feed-through blocks
(`docs/mbr-air-handler-node-plan.md`, `docs/mbr-air-handler-node-shield-review.md`). This board replaces the
proto-shield, the converter and the relay with one fabricated shield, and the same board serves any of the five
air handlers: the zone lives in the sketch and the pivac config, and a ZONE box on the silkscreen takes a marker. The field wiring lands on screw terminals along
one short edge. The 24 VAC is converted on the board through an isolated converter, as on the pivac EXT board, and
the DS18B20 water probes run from a DS2482 I2C master, as on EXT. AC optocouplers read the Y2 and W2 calls, and a
dry-contact input reads the blower. Every part is surface-mount except the terminals, the converter and the Arduino
headers. `docs/air-handler-shield-pcb-layout.svg` is the floor plan, generated by
`docs/air-handler-shield-pcb-layout.gen.py`.

![Air-handler node shield floor plan](air-handler-shield-pcb-layout.svg)

## 1. Scope

**In scope.** One two-layer shield for the UNO R4 WiFi carrying the 24 VAC entry, the rectifier and an isolated
24 VAC to 5.1 V converter feeding the R4's 5V header pin, two AC optocoupler inputs for Y2 and W2, a dry-contact
input for the Unico's blower-running contact, a pulse input reserved for a coil water meter, the two air-temperature
dividers, a DS2482-100 driving the 1-wire bus, status LEDs, a test pad for every bench check and the title block. The generator, the routing
and the gerbers follow the EXT board's toolchain in `hardware/`.

**Out of scope.** The probes, the NTC sensors and their mounting stay as `docs/mbr-air-handler-node-plan.md` §3
specifies them, except that the air sensors no longer need shielded cable (§3). The water meter itself is not chosen.
No change to the Unico controller's settings.

**Boundaries assumed.**

- "Two air flow sensors" means the two 10K NTC air-temperature sensors in the supply and return plenums, which the
  node plan already specifies. A true airflow sensor needs a different front end and a different terminal.
- The field cables land on the shield directly, so the QUATTRO feed-through blocks become optional. The 24 VAC
  terminals take 18 AWG thermostat wire; the sensor terminals take up to 20 AWG.
- Nothing is stacked on this shield. The Arduino header holes take plain male headers.

## 2. Signals at the Unico thermostat block

The Unico Smart Control Board's thermostat block (Bulletin 30-39, Table 5) has these inputs, all 24 VAC from R:

| Terminal | Call | On this board |
|---|---|---|
| R, C | 24 VAC supply | power entry |
| Y1 | cool, low fan | no |
| Y2 | cool, high fan | U3 |
| W1 | heat, low fan | no |
| W2/S1 | heat, high fan | U4 |
| G | fan only | no |
| O-b | reversing valve | no |
| E, W3, S3 | emergency and electric heat | no |

1. **W2.** The thermostats are two-stage in heat and cool, and stage 2 is the air handler's high fan, so W2 is the
   heating twin of Y2. Through the winter, with the Chiltrix on in heat mode, the question the node exists to answer
   (was the coil already on high fan when the room lost ground?) is a W2 question.
2. **Y1, W1, G.** RedLink reports the first-stage calls and the fan-only state, and `HPCALL` confirms the hydronic
   call at the panel. An input here would duplicate them.
3. **O-b.** The `HPHEAT` and `HPCOOL` relays already report the system's mode.
4. **E, W3, S3.** No electric heat is fitted.

The accessories block offers one signal the thermostat side cannot: the EAC contact (Table 7), a dry relay contact
that closes whenever the blower runs on high or low. It proves the blower ran, which separates a dead motor or a
controller fault from a coil that is short of water; air ΔT alone cannot, since a small ΔT means either no air or
little load. It also marks blower runs with no call (fan-only, the controller's run-on), whose supply-air readings are
room air, and counts blower hours. The board reads it on TB2.1 if the EAC terminals are unused. Check that before wiring it:
Diagram 12 shows the EAC contact switching 120 V or 24 V to an air cleaner when one is fitted.

## 3. Board and terminal edge

The shield is 86.36 × 53.34 mm. It follows the R4's outline for 68.58 mm and continues 17.78 mm past the R4's right
edge as a tab with nothing under it. The terminals are Phoenix COMBICON MC 1,5 pluggable screw terminals: an angled header on the tab and a screw
plug that takes the field wires and pulls straight off. A board swaps between air handlers without unscrewing a wire.
The headers stand on the tab with their plugs entering from the right. Inside the R4's outline the right edge is
blocked: the ICSP header stands 8.5 mm tall at mid-height, and the header rows reach the corners.

| Ref | Header | Plug | Positions |
|---|---|---|---|
| TB1 | MC 1,5/4-G-3,81 | MC 1,5/4-ST-3,81 | R · C · Y2 · W2 |
| TB2 | MC 1,5/4-G-3,5 | MC 1,5/4-ST-3,5 | FAN · FLOW · +5 · GND |
| TB3 | MC 1,5/3-G-3,5 | MC 1,5/3-ST-3,5 | SUP · RET · GND |
| TB4 | MC 1,5/3-G-3,5 | MC 1,5/3-ST-3,5 | V · D · G |

1. TB1 is the 24 VAC group, at the top of the edge, 3.81 mm pitch. The sensor groups are 3.5 mm pitch, the smallest
   pitch at which Phoenix makes a pluggable screw plug; its 2.5 mm plugs are spring-cage. The pitch is also the
   guard against the EXT board's plug mix-up (rev C plan §4.1): the 24 VAC plug is 15.2 mm wide and every sensor
   header opening is 14 mm or less, so it seats only on TB1. A sensor plug may enter TB1's wider opening, which
   carries no voltage once its own plug is off, so the danger is the 24 VAC plug alone.
2. TB2 carries the blower-running contact (FAN, from the Unico's EAC terminals, with its other side on GND) and the
   coil water meter, reserved (§4.7): FLOW, +5 and GND.
3. TB3 carries the supply-air and return-air NTCs, their two returns sharing GND. Each divider is a 5 kΩ source with
   100 nF at the pin, and the sketch averages several hundred readings a second, so mains hum averages out. Any
   twisted pair serves: a pair of CAT6 or of thermostat cable. If Belden 8451 is already run, use it and cut the
   drain back at both ends.
4. TB4 is the 1-wire bus to the two DS18B20 water probes, in the V · D · G order of the EXT board's legend.
5. Every MC 1,5 plug takes one conductor of 28–16 AWG, or two of the same size up to 0.5 mm² (20 AWG) in one clamp,
   which is how two returns share a GND position. 18 AWG thermostat cable lands in TB1 directly.
6. TB3 and TB4 are both 3-way 3.5 mm, so their plugs interchange. Either plug in the other's header puts only 5 V
   logic and sensor wiring together, which harms nothing; the silkscreen names each header, and the plugs take a
   marker.
7. The four groups take 51.7 mm of the 53.34 mm edge, the plugs side by side. The headers are through-hole, and a
   plug pulls straight back: never lever it, since one row of pins holds a header, as on the EXT rev A boards. The
   four mounting holes on the UNO pattern take M3 standoffs to the R4; fit the two right-hand standoffs before
   pulling or seating a plug, because they carry the tab.

## 4. Circuit

### 4.1 24 VAC entry and converter

R enters on TB1.1 through F1, a 0.2 A PTC, and D2, a 43 V bidirectional TVS, clamps R to C after the fuse. D1, one
S1M diode beside U1, rectifies half-wave into C1, 100 µF 63 V, which makes VS at about 35 V against C. U1, a Traco
TMR 3-4811WI, takes VS and returns 5.1 V ±1 % across its 1.6 kV isolation barrier.

The EXT board uses a full bridge. This board uses half-wave rectification, which makes C the one reference for the
whole 24 VAC side: the call inputs, VS and the test pad all return to the transformer's C. The board draws about
60 mA at VS, which a single diode carries easily. C1's ripple at that current is 10 V peak to peak, and the valley
at a low line of 21.6 VAC is 19.6 V, above U1's 18 V minimum. A WiFi transmit burst lasts milliseconds, and C1 rides
through it.

U1 is the same family as the EXT board's converter at a quarter of the power. The R4 WiFi draws 0.1–0.45 A at 5 V,
and the TMR 3 delivers 0.6 A. The footprint takes either the TMR 3-4811WI or one of the two TMR 12-4811WI on hand.
Their single-output pins share positions 1, 2, 3, 6, 7 and 8, and the TMR 3's pin 5 has a hole with no copper. The
on-hand pair stays reserved for EXT boards, so buy a TMR 3 for this one.

### 4.2 Feeding the R4

U1's output feeds the R4's 5V header pin directly. The R4 brings USB-C power in through a Schottky diode, which is
why the USB-C feed in PR #240 measured 4.65 V. The header pin gives the full 5.1 V, which is inside the RA4M1's
5.5 V limit and lifts the NTC dividers' reference.

1. **Regulation.** U1 regulates to ±1 % at any line or load the board presents. The R4's own buck sits idle, because
   VIN carries nothing.
2. **Laptop on USB-C while 24 VAC is live.** The same Schottky diode blocks the 5.1 V rail from back-feeding the
   laptop. U1's isolation keeps the laptop's ground off the transformer.
3. **What the header pin gives up.** The pin bypasses the R4's reverse-polarity diode. On a fixed copper trace the
   polarity cannot be reversed, so this costs nothing here.
4. **No USB-C pigtail.** The board has no pigtail socket.

### 4.3 Y2 and W2 inputs

U3 (Y2) and U4 (W2) are Lite-On LTV-814S AC-input optocouplers: two LEDs in inverse parallel driving one
phototransistor, surface-mount, 5 kV between input and output. Each call runs to C through this chain:

| Ref (Y2 / W2) | Part | Job |
|---|---|---|
| R5 R6 / R7 R8 | 2.2 kΩ 1206, in series | set the current |
| LED2 / LED3 | blue / red 0805 | lights on the call |
| D3 / D4 | 1N4148W | carries LED2's reverse half |
| R9 / R10 | 1 kΩ 0805 | across the opto's input |
| U3 / U4 | LTV-814S | the isolation |
| R11 / R12 | 10 kΩ 0805 | pulls D8 or D9 up |
| C7 / C8 | 1 µF 0805 | bridges the zero crossings |

1. At 25.9 VAC the call peaks at 36.6 V and drives 7.6 mA through the 4.4 kΩ string (7.3 mA on Y2, whose blue
   LED drops 3.2 V). R9 takes about 1.2 mA of it,
   and the opto's LEDs carry about 4.5 mA RMS. Each 2.2 kΩ resistor dissipates 0.06 W of its 0.25 W rating.
2. R9 sets a threshold. A current under about 1 mA leaves less than 1 V across the opto's LEDs, which stay dark, so a
   thermostat's off-state leakage cannot read as a call. The 792 relay achieved the same thing with its 19 V
   operate voltage.
3. The LTV-814's transfer ratio is at least 20 %, so 6 mA of LED current gives at least 1.2 mA of collector current
   against the 0.5 mA R11 needs. D8 reads low through the call.
4. The opto lets go for about 1.5 ms around each zero crossing, where the call falls under about 10 V. Through
   R11, C7 rises 0.7 V in that time, so D8 stays low. After the call ends, D8 reads high within 20 ms. The sketch's
   100 ms release hold (node plan §4.6) stays.
5. Each input draws about 5 mA from the thermostat output, as the relay coil did. The SCB input already loads the
   same terminal.
6. The opto replaces a relay, its coil drive and its flyback diode, and it has no contacts to wet or wear.

### 4.4 Blower-running input

TB2.1 takes the EAC contact. R13 pulls the net up to 5 V through 1 kΩ, so the contact carries 5 mA, and LED4 lights
through R14 when it closes. R15 (1 kΩ) and C9 (10 nF) filter the net into A2. The net is a field wire, so the series
resistor limits the current into the pin if anything foreign lands on it.

### 4.5 Air temperatures

R1 and R2 are 10.0 kΩ 0.1 % 25 ppm thin-film 0805s from 5 V to each SIG terminal, with the NTC from SIG to GND.
R3 and R4 (1 kΩ) run from each divider node to A0 and A1, and C5 and C6 (100 nF) sit at the pins. No DC flows
through R3 or R4, so they add no error. The sketch's conversion in node plan §4.5 is unchanged, since the divider
and the ADC reference share the 5 V rail.

### 4.6 1-wire

U2 is a DS2482-100 at address 0x18 (AD0 and AD1 to GND, SLPZ to 5 V), running at 5 V on the R4's SDA and SCL pins,
which are A4 and A5. R18 and R19 (4.7 kΩ) pull SDA and SCL up to 5 V. U2's IO drives TB4.D and supplies the bus
pull-up itself. TB4.V comes from 5 V through F2, a second 0.2 A PTC, so a shorted probe cable trips F2 before it
drops the R4's rail. C4 decouples U2.

JP1 is the rollback, open as built. Closed, it joins the bus to D2, and R20 (4.7 kΩ, not fitted) becomes the
bit-banged pull-up the present sketch uses. Leave U2 off the board while running that way.

### 4.7 Coil water meter (reserved)

TB2 carries FLOW, +5 and GND for a pulse meter. R16 (10 kΩ) pulls SIG up, and R17 (100 Ω) with C10 (1 nF) feeds A3,
which can raise an interrupt on the R4. Fit the three parts when a meter is chosen (Appendix E.6 of the cooling
assessment).

### 4.8 Pin map

| R4 pin | Net |
|---|---|
| 5V | U1 output |
| GND | ground |
| A0 | return air |
| A1 | supply air |
| A2 | FAN |
| A3 | FLOW |
| A4 / SDA | U2 SDA |
| A5 / SCL | U2 SCL |
| D8 | Y2 |
| D9 | W2 |
| D2 | JP1 only |

1. D8 keeps the Y2 pin the present sketch reads.
2. D0 and D1 (serial), D4 and D5 (CAN) and D10 to D13 (SPI) stay unused. The ICSP header passes through six
   clearance holes with no copper.
3. A3 replaces the node plan's D3 for the flow meter, so each input lands on the header row nearest its terminal.

## 5. Layout

The board is laid out in bands, each running from its terminals on the tab to the Arduino pins it serves.

1. **Top band, 24 VAC.** The entry (F1, D2) sits beside TB1. The Y2 and W2 chains run left from TB1 in two rows
   to U3 and U4, whose outputs face D8 and D9 on the header above. R and C cross to the top-left on the solder side,
   between the two rows, to D1, C1 and U1, which stand under the left half of the digital header. U1's input pins
   face C1, and its output pins face the left edge.
2. **Left edge, 5 V.** U1's output capacitors, the PWR LED and the +5 and GND test pads sit above the R4's USB-C.
   The 5 V track runs down the left to the 5V header pin.
3. **Middle band.** The FAN chain runs left from TB2 above the ICSP holes, and the FLOW chain below them.
   Both turn down to A2 and A3.
4. **Lower band.** The air dividers stand in two rows above A0 and A1. The DS2482 group sits on the tab beside TB4.
5. **Title block.** The INT board's block, at full scale, in the bottom-left over the R4's barrel jack.

| Ref | Part | Centre (x, y) |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | TMR 3-4811WI, SIP-8 | body 3.0–24.8, 37.6–46.8 |
| C1 | 100 µF 63 V, 10 × 10.2 | 31.5, 42.3 |
| D1 | S1M | 33.5, 35.0 |
| F1, D2 | entry | 75.5 / 70.0, 50.3 |
| U3, U4 | LTV-814S | 57.5, 46.0 / 40.3 |
| U2 | DS2482-100, SOIC-8 | 72.5, 4.8 |
| R1–R4, C5, C6 | air dividers | 41.5–49.5, 11.0 / 15.5 |
| TP1, TP2 | VS, C | 25.6 / 28.4, 35.0 |
| TP3 | R after F1 | 65.6, 50.3 |
| J1 | test row, 13 pads | 17.0–47.5, 26.0 |
| title block | ring and DL | ring centre 8.6, 21.0 |

1. The barrel jack under the bottom-left corner stands 11 mm, and the shield rests on it. Nothing through-hole goes
   there, and polyimide tape goes on the shield's underside over it.
2. U1's pins pass over the R4's USB-C, which stands 3.2 mm. Clip them to 1.5 mm.
3. U1 needs a rule area with no copper under its body (Traco's datasheet), as on EXT rev C.
4. The test pads follow EXT rev C §9 and §5.1 below: every check has a pad on the component side, so the board
   proves itself before the R4 goes on and can be diagnosed in place with the R4 on.
5. The title block is `title_block()` in `hardware/gen-boards.py` at scale 1.0: the DL monogram of
   `hardware/dl-monogram.svg` in its ring, "PIVAC MONITORING BOARD" in 1.45 mm bold over a rule, and
   "v1.0  Rev A  AIR HANDLER" in 1.05 mm, with a ZONE box beside it, all in KiCad's stroke font as on INT and EXT.
6. The pin-1 legends, the TB labels and a `24VAC input` legend at TB1 follow the EXT boards' conventions.

### 5.1 Test pads

TP1 to TP3 are plated loops on the 24 VAC side, sized for a probe hook. J1 is a row of thirteen plated pads at
2.54 mm on the 5 V side, labelled on the silkscreen, which also takes a 1 × 13 pin header for a logic analyser.

| Pad | Net |
|---|---|
| TP1 | VS |
| TP2 | C |
| TP3 | R after F1 |
| J1.1, J1.13 | GND |
| J1.2 | +5 |
| J1.3 | Y2, D8 |
| J1.4 | W2, D9 |
| J1.5 | FAN, A2 |
| J1.6 | FLOW, A3 |
| J1.7 | air return, A0 |
| J1.8 | air supply, A1 |
| J1.9 | 1-wire V, after F2 |
| J1.10 | 1-wire DATA |
| J1.11 | SDA |
| J1.12 | SCL |

| Check | Between | Expect |
|---|---|---|
| Transformer | TB1 R and C | 24–28 VAC |
| F1 | TP3 and TP2 | as above; 0 V means F1 tripped |
| VS | TP1 and TP2 | about 35 V DC |
| U1 output | J1.2 and J1.1 | 5.1 V |
| Isolation | TP2 and J1.1 | open, R4 unplugged from USB |
| Y2 input | J1.3 and J1.1 | under 0.5 V on a call, 5 V idle |
| W2 input | J1.4 and J1.1 | as Y2 |
| FAN input | J1.5 and J1.1 | under 0.5 V while the blower runs |
| FLOW input | J1.6 and J1.1 | pulses with flow |
| Air dividers | J1.7, J1.8 and J1.1 | 2.5 V at 77 °F |
| F2 | J1.9 and J1.1 | 5 V; 0 V means a shorted probe cable |
| 1-wire bus | J1.10 and J1.1 | 5 V idle, pulses while reading |
| I2C | J1.11, J1.12 and J1.1 | 5 V idle |

1. The isolation check reads TP2 against GND with the meter on ohms and the board unpowered. A laptop on the R4's
   USB-C grounds GND to the laptop, which does not affect the reading but adds a path to check.
2. A Y2 or W2 call that lights LED2 or LED3 but leaves J1.3 or J1.4 at 5 V points at the optocoupler or its output
   capacitor. A call at the TB1 screws with LED2 dark points at R5, R6 or the LED string.
3. An air divider at 5 V means an open sensor or cable, and at 0 V a short.

## 7. Firmware and pivac

The board changes the sketch in four places. Everything else in `AirHandler_impl.h` stays.

1. **Water temperatures** read through the DS2482 over `Wire` at 0x18, using a DS2482 driver that presents the
   OneWire interface `DallasTemperature` expects, or Adafruit's DS248x library with its own DS18B20 read. Bring-up
   mode prints the ROMs as before. With JP1 closed and U2 removed, the present D2 code runs unchanged.
2. **W2** on D9 gets the same treatment as Y2: `w2` now, and `w2_s` as seconds since boot.
3. **FAN** on A2, read digitally and published as `fan` (1 while the blower runs) and `fan_s`.
4. **FLOW** on A3 waits for a meter.

On the pivac side, the `pivac.UnicoAH_MBR` block gains `w2`, `w2_s`, `fan` and `fan_s` inputs, publishing
`environment.inside.hvac.ah.mbr.w2`, `.w2Seconds`, `.fan` and `.fanSeconds`. PR #240 carries the rest of the
node's pivac side unchanged.

## 8. Issues and concerns

1. **Confirm the Unico terminals carry what §2 says.** With a meter on the SCB's thermostat block, read W2 to C
   during a second-stage heat call and Y2 to C during a second-stage cool call: 24 VAC on the call, near zero
   otherwise. A thermostat wired single-stage to the SCB leaves W2 dead.
2. **The EAC contact must be unused.** If an air cleaner, ERV or UV lamp hangs on it, TB2.1 would see 24 or 120 VAC.
   Leave FAN unwired in that case.
3. **Transformer capacity.** The SCB's transformer is 50 VA (Unico part A00057). The board adds about 3 VA: 2 W at
   the converter's input at full WiFi load, plus 5 mA per active call input. Read the nameplate.
4. **The tab carries terminal torque.** Fit both right-hand standoffs before landing wires, and support the tab with
   a finger while tightening.
5. **The ICSP and barrel-jack positions come from the UNO standard and Arduino's R4 WiFi drawing.** Check both
   against the board in hand before generating the footprint: set an R4 under a 1:1 print of the floor plan.
6. **The firmware has to change for U2.** Until the DS2482 code is proven, the board can run with JP1 closed and U2
   off on the present sketch.
7. **The 1-wire bus runs at 5 V.** Both the DS2482 and the probes take 5 V; the bus never meets 3.3 V.
8. **The new pin map moves the flow meter from D3 to A3**, and the node plan's §3.4 and §4 tables need updating when
   this board is built.
9. **Part availability is unconfirmed** (§6).
