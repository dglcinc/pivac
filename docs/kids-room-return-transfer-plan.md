# Kids room return transfer plan

2026-09-16

A lined, staggered return transfer in the wall of the closed bedroom will let its Unico outlets deliver their full airflow, so the room tracks the zone setpoint instead of running warm. Build it in one stud bay with the grilles offset 36 in. vertically (24 in. minimum), lined with half-inch rigid fiberglass duct liner, and sized so the grilles run under 300 fpm.

## 1. The problem

The room is on the kids-room zone (Unico M1218 air handler, chilled and hot water from the buffer tank). Its door is kept closed, the clearance under it is tight, and the room has no return grille.

With the door closed the room has no way to let supply air out except through leaks into the wall cavities and ceiling. The room pressurizes, each outlet delivers a fraction of its 35 to 45 CFM, and the air that does arrive short-circuits out through the gaps. The thermostat sits elsewhere in the zone and reads satisfied, so the occupant's complaint never reaches it. The closed room also misses its share of the zone's dehumidification, and this zone is the most humid in the house.

**Diagnostic before building.** Leave the door open for a day or two. If the complaint goes away, the transfer is the fix. If the room still runs warm with the door open, it has a load problem (sun, electronics, too few outlets) and the transfer helps less.

## 2. Design

A staggered transfer inside one stud bay of the bedroom wall: a grille on the room face and a grille on the hall face, offset vertically so there is no line of sight, with the bay between them lined in half-inch rigid fiberglass duct liner. Air enters the room-side grille, drops through the lined bay, and leaves through the hall-side grille.

Light needs only no line of sight, so any offset beyond the grille height blocks it. Sound is a length question: half-inch liner in a small duct takes about 5 to 10 dB per foot out of the speech band and 1 to 3 dB per foot below 250 Hz, and each lined turn adds a few dB.

| Offset between grille centres | Light | Speech privacy |
|---|---|---|
| 12 in. | blocked | poor, a few dB over an open pair |
| 24 in. | blocked | close to the wall's own rating, adequate |
| 36 in. or more | blocked | matches the wall, recommended |

At 24 in. the transfer has about two feet of lined path and two turns, which takes a straight grille pair from 10 to 15 dB of attenuation to the low 30s, about what a 2x4 drywall wall does on its own. At 36 in. the path runs close to the wall's rating across the speech band, and the extra foot costs nothing inside a stud bay. Below the speech band the door's mass matters more than the offset, so the transfer will not beat the door.

Half-inch liner is the right choice at 3.5 in. bay depth. One-inch liner attenuates about half again as much, but it leaves only 1.5 in. of clear path, so the longer offset at half an inch is the better trade.

## 3. Sizing

Count the room's outlets and allow about 40 CFM each. Two limits apply: the free area must hold the pressure difference across the door under about 0.03 in. w.c. (about 700 fpm through the free area), and the grille faces should run under 300 fpm so the transfer itself is silent. The second limit governs, so the grilles are about twice the free-area minimum.

| Room supply | Free area minimum | Grille size at each end (about 60 % free area, under 300 fpm) |
|---|---|---|
| 80 CFM (2 outlets) | 17 in² | 10 × 6 |
| 120 CFM (3 outlets) | 25 in² | 10 × 8 or 12 × 6 |
| 160 CFM (4 outlets) | 34 in² | 12 × 8 or 14 × 6 |

Size the bay's clear section for the lined dimension: half-inch liner takes an inch off each interior dimension, so a 3.5 in. bay lined leaves 2.5 in. by 13.5 in., about 34 in² of clear path, which carries 120 CFM at about 500 fpm. For a four-outlet room use two bays or a wider box.

An undercut door is not a substitute: a 1 in. gap under a 30 in. door is 30 in² gross with a poor flow coefficient and no attenuation.

## 4. Construction

- Grilles on opposite faces of the wall, offset so there is no straight line of sight. Put the room-side grille high if summer is the concern, since the warm air collects there, and the hall-side grille low.
- Line all four faces of the bay between the grilles with half-inch rigid fiberglass duct liner, faced or coated side toward the air, edges sealed with duct-liner adhesive or foil tape so no fibres reach the airstream.
- Block the bay top and bottom beyond the grilles so the transfer is a closed box, and seal it to the drywall so air cannot escape into the rest of the wall.
- Use stamped or bar grilles with no damper; a filter is not needed.
- Check the interior wall for wiring, plumbing and fire blocking before cutting. The wall is an interior partition, so no fire rating is at issue.

## 5. What changes and what does not

The occupant's room will track the zone setpoint, and its humidity will fall toward the rest of the zone's, because supply air that now leaks into the wall cavities comes back across the coil.

The air handler's calling pattern stays where it is. The zone's total load does not change, so the thermostat's duty and the 2 cycles per hour setting produce the same calls. Two small shifts: the blower sees a little less static and delivers a few percent more air across the zone, and the closed room takes its design share, so the rooms near the thermostat get slightly less. Runs may lengthen by a minute or so at the same duty. The coil's ΔT and the zone's humidity readings should improve a little. Nothing on the water loop or the chiller notices.

If the room has its own load problem, the transfer fixes the delivery deficit and the room will still run a degree or two above the thermostat's room on the worst afternoons; the next step then is an added outlet.
