Accepted, because `IN − OUT` is what that pair exists to measure and a pair correction moved at most 0.101 °F across 45–140 °F on the probes that were measured at three points. The **pair** ΔT-zero corrections are what matter for the loops: −0.87 °F on loop A, +0.97 °F on loop B, −0.585 °F on the primary, which against 2–6 °F deltas is 15–50%. The loop figures are large because each loop crosses two bench pairs. **⚠️ Calibrate near the operating point — a better-controlled bath does not compensate for distance.** Applying a 100 °F offset at 45 °F costs up to 0.316 °F while the ice offset costs 0.043, because 45 is 13 degrees from the ice point and 55 from the circulator. **The third point mattered for winter, not summer:** it moved every 45 °F offset by ≤0.014 K but moved the 140 °F offsets by up to 0.157 K, since two points cannot be extrapolated 40 degrees past the last anchor. **Heating-season (140 °F) offsets are recorded in a `config.yml` comment** — LOOPA_SUP +0.346, LOOPA_RET +0.220, LOOPB_SUP +0.029, LOOPB_RET +0.152 — and swapping to them is the winter fix if absolute loop temperatures start to matter. **⚠️ Pair ΔT needs them too**, which was not true while each loop held one bench pair: matched-slope pairs moved their ΔT correction under 0.101 °F across 45–140 °F, but the crossed pairs move 0.64 °F on loop A and 0.74 °F on loop B. **⚠️ Absolute offsets are only as good as the Thermapen, ±0.5 °F, and the pair differences are the part that reproduces.** A constant thermometer bias cancels from the slope entirely — reading every bath 0.10 °F low shifts every offset by −0.10 and changes no slope, no pair correction and no ΔT. **Linearity was tested rather than assumed** and holds to 0.15 °F, with the departure being per-part curvature rather than a bad bath: a wrong anchor displaces every probe identically, yet the mean residual is five times smaller than the per-probe values and the sign differs between probes. Re-measuring PA1 and PA2 a week apart moved every one of the four by +0.087…+0.162 °F, mean +0.134 — a uniform shift, so the two baths' reference readings disagreed (32.05 vs 32.10 °F anchors) rather than four sensors drifting together. A shared bath error cancels out of a pair difference, which is why PA1's reproduced to 0.021 °F. It also cancels between the two families above, so mixing anchors costs nothing in any ΔT. **PA2 reproduced only to 0.058 °F and PA2A is the noisiest probe measured (SD 0.060)** — it is one of the two that came off the wall thermally decoupled, and it now serves as `LOOPB_SUP`, so re-window it before trusting loop B's ΔT correction. **`IN − OUT` therefore steps by +0.325 K (+0.585 °F) at that date** — roughly a tenth of the 5–6 °F primary ΔT — so a ΔT comparison spanning it must account for the step, as it must for the 2026-08-18 precision change. The calibration is **ice-point only**; slope is unverified, which matters because the loop runs near 45 °F rather than 32 °F. **⚠️ `presence=0` and `presence=1 roms=0` are different faults, and the distinction is the fastest diagnostic on this bus.** Presence with a failed ROM search means devices are alive and the bit timing is marginal — the search reads 15 µs windows and fails first. No presence at all means nothing sees the bus: power, a conductor clamped on its jacket, or a broken lead. That test retired `0516a36332ff` (the former IN) on 2026-08-29 — silent alone on a known-good bus after its cap seal was repaired with heat shrink, so its leads are presumed broken at the cap. **⚠️ The Arduino bench rig needs 5 V on BOTH the probe supply and the pull-up.** The UNO R4 runs its logic at 5 V, so a 3.3 V pull-up idles the data line below the input-high threshold. The signature is unmistakable and cost an hour on 2026-08-29: the bus answers a reset with a **presence pulse but the ROM search returns nothing** — `presence=1 roms=0` — because a presence pulse is one long low and the search reads individual bits in ~15 µs windows. 3.3 V is right for the **Pi's** bus and wrong here. **Verify an offset by reading `/sys` and the Signal K value in the same command** — sampled seconds apart, loop drift swamps the correction. Expect agreement to within one DS18B20 LSB (0.0625 °C), since the two reads are separate conversions. **⚠️ The DHW recirc DS18B20 is measured but its correction cannot be applied yet (2026-08-29)**: **its offset is +0.274 K, and that value is already parked on the `0316a015e7ff` entry in `/etc/pivac/config.yml`, ready to go live the moment the probe joins the Pi's 1-wire bus.** **It is stated at 120 °F, the recirc-loop temperature, deliberately NOT at the 45 °F used for LOOPA/LOOPB** — an offset is only right near the temperature it was interpolated to, and this probe works 75 degrees from the chilled loop. Do not "make it consistent" with the loop values. The probe hangs off the Arduino at 10.0.0.114 and reaches Signal K through `pivac.ArduinoSensor`, and **that module has no `offset` support at all** — the key exists only in `OneWireTherm`. So `environment.inside.hvac.dhw.recirc.temperature` still carries the full ±0.9 °F sensor spec. **The general rule this is an instance of: pick each probe's offset from the column matching its operating temperature**, which is why UBT/LBT keep ice-point values, LOOPA/LOOPB use 45 °F, and this one uses 120 °F. The per-temperature table is in `docs/ds18b20-PA1-5-calibration.md`. The three decommissioned probes were calibrated in the same run and are now usable spares: `0316a00f04ff` +0.153, `0516a36816ff` +0.496, `0516a365d8ff` +0.056 K. `0516a36332ff`, the former IN, is still uncalibrated — it would not enumerate. **Diagnostic gotcha worth knowing:** the printed DS18B20 label is the **full 8-byte ROM** (e.g. `28FF1668` + `A316054F` = `28FF1668A316054F`) while the Linux w1 device name **reverses the 6 serial bytes** → `28-0516a36816ff`; verify a label with a CRC-8/Maxim check over the first 7 bytes before assuming a sensor is new. **The printed tags are NOT reliable as physical identifiers (found 2026-08-10):** one probe was carrying **two** tags, the extra being OUT's `28FFD865A3160552` (= `28-0516a365d8ff`). Since the ROM is read from the chip itself, the w1 bus is always authoritative and no data is at risk — but to determine *which physical probe* is which, unplug it and watch which `28-*` entry leaves `/sys/bus/w1/devices/`; do not read the label. The probe with a history of intermittently dropping off the bus (a ~7.5 h dropout on 2026-05-31) is the **retired** `0516a365d8ff`, so the same pattern on the present OUT would indict the cable or the bus rather than the chip. **Retiring a name requires a `signalk` restart:** Signal K keeps a retired path frozen at its last value, so a sensor that published under its raw ID before being named in config still sits in the API after `restart pivac-1wire` alone. Recipe: **config edit → `restart pivac-1wire` → `restart signalk`.** Note `environment.outside` 404s for ~80 s after the signalk restart until RedLink completes its ~75 s re-login — the documented cold-start path, not a failure. InfluxDB keeps orphaned measurements from every past rename with their history; `IN`, `OUT`, `UBT` and `LBT` all carry continuous history across their probe swaps because the **names** did not change, only the ROMs behind them. **Check the sign after any swap of a pair** — OUT runs about 5–6 °F warmer than IN, verified across the 2026-08-23 change, and a reversed pair would silently invert the capacity measurement without going stale.

## Mounting

### How to mount a secondary-loop pair so its ΔT means something

The dominant error is heat leaking off the probe body and leads to ambient air, not sensor accuracy, so mounting decides whether a pair's ΔT is worth reading. **Mount on copper at the tees, never on PEX.**

- Lay the stainless probe **axially along the pipe** for full-body contact, bedded in thermal grease and held down with a worm clamp or copper tape. A probe standing proud of the pipe is measuring the room.
- Give the cable a **2–3 in thermal lead-in** along the pipe, under the insulation, before it leaves — otherwise the leads conduct the probe toward ambient.
- **Insulate about 10 pipe diameters (≈4–6 in) of copper each side of the probe.** That span is what holds the sensor's zone at water temperature.
- Pick a straight run just downstream of a tee or elbow, where flow is mixed, and away from high points where air collects.
- **Strain-relieve the cable to the pipe an inch or two behind the probe.** This is the fix for the decoupling failure below: a cable tug slides the tube out from under its clamp while the zip-tied insulation stays visibly intact, so inspection finds nothing.

**Put the supply probe after the zone pump**, which is the true coil inlet. Pump self-heat (~0.1 °F at 5 GPM and 90 W, `ΔT = P/(ṁ·cp)`) is then counted in supply and correctly excluded from coil load. The return probe goes at the coil outlet.

**Mount both probes of a pair identically.** ΔT is the measurement, so residual conduction error only cancels when it is common-mode across the pair — two probes mounted differently leave a bias that no calibration offset removes.

## Bus faults

### A w1 bus that reports `max_slave_count 64 reached` has an electrical fault, not 64 sensors — RESOLVED 2026-08-25 by dropping the pull-up to 2.2 kΩ

connecting the four secondary-loop probes took the bus from 4 healthy devices to **zero** — including IN and OUT, which had been fine for six days. The kernel logged `w1_slave_driver 28-000000cc0c90: w1_therm_add_slave: Device has been added, but resolution may be corrupted. err=-5` and, four seconds later, `w1_master_driver w1_bus_master1: w1_search: max_slave_count 64 reached, will continue next search`. **The 64 is the tell.** The search algorithm walks the ROM tree bit by bit; when bit timing is unreliable it takes both branches at every node and manufactures phantom devices until it hits the 64 ceiling, then settles at a `w1_master_slave_count` of 0. **Read the pin before assuming a short:** `raspi-gpio get 4` (GPIO 4 is the default for `dtoverlay=w1-gpio` with no `gpiopin=`) showed `level=1`, so the line idled high and the pull-up was alive — signalling integrity, not a dead short. Ranked causes when the device count doubles: **one 4.7 kΩ pull-up no longer driving the added cable capacitance** (drop it to ~2.2 kΩ, or parallel a second 4.7 kΩ), **star topology** (1-wire wants a daisy chain with short stubs), and **a probe with DQ and VDD swapped** (stainless-probe wire colours are not consistent between batches). **The bisect is the only decisive test:** remove the added probes, confirm the original set re-enumerates, then add back one at a time watching `cat /sys/bus/w1/devices/w1_bus_master1/w1_master_slave_count`. One probe killing it every time is that probe or its wiring; failing only past a certain device count is electrical loading. **Note the failure is silent in pivac** — `pivac-1wire` stays `active` and logs only `OneWireTherm bus now has 0 sensor(s)`, and the 30-minute freshness alerts are what actually reported it (UBT/LBT emailed at 22:45 and resolved at 23:05). **The Pi's bus runs at 3.3 V throughout, so the `0.7 × VDD` input-threshold failure mode is ruled out here** — it is worth knowing only because `docs/ds18b20-provisioning.md` specifies 5 V for the Arduino bench rig, where it is correct, and carrying that across would put a 3.5 V threshold on a 3.3 V bus. With 3.3 V confirmed and the pull-up alive, the three causes above are the whole list, and the four-clean/eight-dead pattern points at cable capacitance against pull-up strength. **Wiring rules and the DS2482 migration are written up in `docs/ds18b20-bus-topology.md`**: trunk topology, DQ paired with GND on one twisted pair, the single 2.2 kΩ pull-up at the master, the 100 Ω per-branch series mitigation when the star has to stay, and the verified DS2482 procedure (`ds2482.ko` ships in the running kernel and binds `ds2484` too, `active_pullup` defaults on, no overlay ships so the device is instantiated over sysfs and pinned with a systemd unit, `i2c_arm` is currently `off`, live config is `/boot/firmware/config.txt`). pivac needs no change either way — the `28-*` names come from the chips. **Root cause, measured 2026-08-24: cable capacitance against pull-up strength, and the arithmetic closes to the digit.** Main loop **1.75 nF**, outdoor run **1.95 nF** (bare cable, no probe fitted). 45.5 ft of conductor — 15 ft Pi to first header, 1.5 ft and 3 ft between the three headers, a 10 ft stub to the tank, 16 ft of untrimmed probe lead — is 13.9 m, which at 112 pF/m is 1.55 nF, plus 200 pF for eight DS18B20 pins at 25 pF each. **⚠️ The 9 µs rise budget is the *guaranteed* worst case, not the operative one.** A DS18B20 may sample a write slot anywhere from 15 to 60 µs after the falling edge and only the 15 µs end is promised; real parts sample near 30 µs, giving ~24 µs. Against 24 µs the history fits: the configuration that ran for months needed **20.9 µs** and the one that collapsed needed **24.3 µs**, either side of the threshold — which is also why the failure was total rather than gradual, since crossing it corrupts every bit of the ROM search at once. **Design to 9 µs; expect failure near 24.** **The cable runs 112 pF/m**, 2.2× CAT5e, because jacketed 3-conductor puts DQ between VDD and GND with *both* at AC ground while a twisted pair gives it one grounded neighbour. Cable *category* never matters — `C = 1/(Z₀·v)` forces CAT5e and CAT6 both to ~50 pF/m — the **pairing** does, so leave spare conductors floating and never ground one beside DQ. **Topology was never the problem:** three Phoenix Contact **ST 1,5/S QUATTRO** headers distributed along the run, trunk in and out, probes on the spare terminals, which is a chain. RC binds this bus, not reflection (a round trip down 40 m settles in a few hundred ns, far inside the sample point), so the 100 Ω damping resistors were never needed. **Bus health is measurable, not just "it enumerates":** CRC per read (`w1_slave` ends `crc=XX YES/NO`), search stability (`w1_master_slave_count` must never dip — the ROM search is the most timing-critical operation and fails first), and `ext_power` (1 = externally powered, 0 = VDD not reaching that probe). Verified after the fix: **320 reads, 0 CRC failures, 0 sweeps returning other than 8 devices, all eight `ext_power=1` at 12-bit.** **Two debugging traps:** a voltage check **cannot** tell DQ from VDD, since both idle at 3.3 V and the pull-up between them reads the same either way — load each to GND through 1 kΩ, VDD holds 3.3 V and DQ sags to ~1.0 V; and `attempts` incrementing with `slave_count` at 0 and **no** phantom-64 message means nothing is answering at all, i.e. connectivity or power, not rise time. **Remaining headroom is free:** the eight untrimmed 2 ft probe leads are 16 ft of the 45.5 ft total, so trimming them and taking out trunk slack cuts ~37% and is most of what would put the outdoor probe back on this bus.

## Probes that read wrong without going stale

### A thermally decoupled DS18B20 publishes fresh, plausible, wrong data forever — detect it with swing and correlation, not freshness (2026-08-25)

two of the four secondary-loop probes settled at ~64 °F while the water ran 51–57, and every alert stayed green because a probe that has lost contact with the pipe still reports its own die temperature accurately on a clean CRC. **A DS18B20 cannot report a wrong temperature** — it reports where its die actually is, so a plausible-but-wrong number is always a mounting or position problem, never a wiring or chip fault. Genuine chip failure looks different and is unmistakable: **85.0 °C exactly** (the power-on reset value, returned when a conversion never completes), the ±127.9 / −0.06 rails, or CRC errors. **Two measurements find it, both free.** *Swing*: over any window with pump cycling, a coupled probe moves 7–14 °F while a decoupled one moves 1–2. *Pairwise correlation*: a loop's own supply and return read **−0.02** against each other (impossible for two probes on the same flowing loop), while the two decoupled probes correlated **0.89 with each other** and 0.34 with the water — the signature of two sensors sitting in the same still air. Cross-check against a trusted probe: a loop return cannot read 9 °F above the combined `OUT` it flows into. **The likely mechanical cause is a cable tug**, which slides the probe tube out from under a worm clamp while the zip-tied insulation stays visibly intact, so inspection finds nothing — **strain-relieve the cable to the pipe an inch or two behind the probe.** The timing is the tell: both failed in the same 30-minute bucket during normal operation, and two chips do not fail simultaneously while two cables in one bundle do. **Ice-test to settle chip-versus-mounting** — 32.0 °F exonerates the chip. **The same correlation test finds probes cross-connected between loops, a fault decoupling hides** (2026-08-29). Once all four were coupled and swinging 9–16 °F, the strongest pairs were `LOOPA_SUP`↔`LOOPB_RET` at **+0.99** and `LOOPA_RET`↔`LOOPB_SUP` at **+0.95**, while the two *labelled* pairs were the weakest of the six at +0.73 and +0.62 — the supplies were on each other's loops. Sign alone catches it as well: loop A read `RET − SUP` of **−10.6 °F** and loop B **+16.4 °F**, where a cooling loop runs a few degrees positive and an idle one near zero. **Group by measured correlation, never by the label**, and check the sign of every pair after any install.

### An idle loop's ΔT is fiction, and the inside tees are the dangerous ones (measured 2026-08-29)

a secondary's probes sit in a short dead leg off the closely spaced tees, and with its pump off the pair does not converge — the supply leg hangs off the cold half of the header and the return leg off the warm half, so it holds a plausible positive ΔT indefinitely. **Loop A reads +3.6 °F with its pump off, against a genuine +5.0 °F of its own**, and nothing in the trace marks it false. **The contamination is asymmetric and follows the header geometry.** Loop A's supply and return tee are the *inside* pair, closest to the primary mains: its supply probe tracks `IN` to **within 0.2 °F in every state, idle included**, so it is effectively a second `IN` probe and **no test on that probe can tell whether loop A's own pump is running**. Loop B's tees are outboard, in a section of header that is nearly dead when only loop A draws, so both its probes stagnate together and its ΔT falls to +1.1 °F honestly. **The primary is the mild case** — `IN` and `OUT` sit close together on the same header and drift to the same ambient, so `OUT − IN` does decay toward zero when idle (+0.3 to −0.1 measured over a quiet morning), but zero is then ambiguous between no flow and no load. **Consequence: gate every loop ΔT on a switch, never on a probe** — `pivac.LoopDelta` does this. **The control chain is `zone → HZ432 zone → equipment Y → Taco` (the `CHIL` relay), and the HZ432 separately opens that zone's valve and starts its secondary pump.** So `CHIL` proves the primary is pumping but asserts for a call on *either* loop, which is exactly the state that fabricates the other loop's ΔT — a secondary needs its zones too. `CHIL` agrees with the union of the three chiller zones **95.6%** of the time over 10 h on a 30 s grid, the disagreement sitting entirely on the edges: **the RedLink zone state arrives a median 48 s after the relay**, one being a local GPIO read every 0.5 s and the other Honeywell's cloud. **The first minute of a run is not a measurement either** — the probes still hold the stagnant values they drifted to with the pump off — but past that the live value is what to plot, charge-up included: ΔT overshoots to **+6.2 °F a minute in against a settled +4.0**, and that shape is real. Zones short-cycle (`KIDS_ROOM` runs 7–13 min calls on a ~20 min period), so a longer trim would eat much of each call for no gain. **⚠️ Do not aggregate a short-cycling signal into buckets near its own period** — a first pass at this used 10-minute means against a ~20-minute cycle and read the beat rather than the data.

## Precision of stored temperatures

### 1-wire temperatures are stored in Kelvin, so `rounding` is a precision setting, not a cosmetic one (fixed 2026-08-18)

`pivac.OneWireTherm` reads `Unit.KELVIN` whenever the output is Signal K (`pivac/OneWireTherm.py:99`) and *then* applies the config's `rounding`. With the historical `rounding: 0` that made every 1-wire value an **integer Kelvin — 1.8 °F granularity**, coarser than the DS18B20's own 0.0625 °C resolution. Absolute readings tolerated it; **differences did not.** A ΔT built from two sensors moves in 1.8 °F steps with up to ±1.8 °F error — ~18 % on a 10 °F hydronic loop delta, and ~45 % on the ~4 °F primary delta across the closely spaced tees, which is what makes `IN − OUT` quantitatively useless at `rounding: 0`. Now **`rounding: 2`** (0.018 °F), set at the top level of the `pivac.OneWireTherm` block so it propagates to every sensor. **Safe to change:** the InfluxDB `_value` column is already `double` (verified live), so no measurement is orphaned, no field-type conflict occurs, and no `signalk` restart is needed — only `restart pivac-1wire`. **Not retroactive:** history before this date stays quantized, so any ΔT analysis must start from here. If WilhelmSK gauges start showing noisy decimals, fix the precision in the app — do not put `rounding` back to 0.

### `pivac.RedLink` had the same defect, and worse — it *truncated* (fixed 2026-08-18)

`sk_add_value(..., int(ktemp))` (`pivac/RedLink.py:329`, plus the outdoor path) emitted **whole Kelvin**, and `int()` truncates rather than rounds — so every zone temperature read **up to 1.8 °F cold, always downward, mean ≈0.9 °F**. That is a *systematic bias*, not just coarseness, and it lands on the one number the comfort work depends on: **droop (`temperature − coolset`) was understated**, so rooms drift further than the history shows. Seen live: `MASTER_BR` stored 297 K (75.2 °F) against `coolset` 76 — reading "below setpoint" when the true value could have been 76.9 °F, i.e. *above* it. Setpoints were truncated too (`int(float(...))`), discarding Honeywell's half-degree steps and biasing the same direction. Now `round(ktemp, 2)` for temperatures and `round(..., 1)` for setpoints. **Safe:** the InfluxDB `_value` for `environment.inside.thermostat.*` and `environment.outside.thermostat.*` is already `double` (verified live), so no orphaning and no field-type conflict; `restart pivac-redlink` only — but expect the documented **~75 s cold-start login** before values reappear, which is not a failure. **Not retroactive.**

## Module behaviour

### OneWireTherm SensorNotReadyError

1-wire sensors occasionally not ready mid-conversion. Transient, self-recovering.

### OneWireTherm per-sensor isolation (FIXED 2026-05-31, PR pending)

`pivac.OneWireTherm.status()` now wraps the per-sensor read in a try/except inside the `for sensor in sensors` loop (same isolation pattern as RedLink `_refresh_one`). A transient `SensorNotReadyError` (or any per-sensor failure) is logged as a WARNING (`OneWireTherm sensor <id> read failed (...); skipping this cycle`) and the loop `continue`s, so the healthy sensors still publish. **Prior behaviour (the bug):** any one DS18B20 throwing mid-cycle bubbled up to `pivac-provider.py:171`'s module-level catch, the whole cycle was skipped, and NO 1-wire values published — all three hydronic water-temp gauges (In/CRW/Out) went stale together even when only one sensor was bad. Confirmed live 2026-05-31: sensor `0516a365d8ff` (OUT) dropped off the w1 bus and silenced IN/CRW/AMB for ~7.5h; after the weekly reboot it re-enumerated and reads normally (the dropout was intermittent, not a dead sensor). Note this isolates the *per-cycle read*; the older crash-loop on `NoSensorFoundError` at module import (when the bus is so unreliable a configured sensor won't instantiate at all) is a separate failure mode still recovered only by reboot — `sensors = W1ThermSensor.get_available_sensors()` runs once at import (line 19).

### OneWireTherm bus is now re-scanned every cycle — boot-race empty enumeration self-heals (FIXED 2026-07-06, PR pending)

`pivac.OneWireTherm` used to scan the w1 bus **once at import** (`sensors = W1ThermSensor.get_available_sensors()`, line 19). The kernel takes a few seconds to enumerate the DS18B20s after a cold boot, but the systemd service starts immediately — so on essentially **every power cycle** the module cached an empty/partial sensor list and ran `active` but silently published nothing until a manual `restart pivac-1wire`. Root-caused live 2026-07-06 (power cycle 11:41:55 → service started 11:42:13 → all 1-wire SK paths stale, no journal errors). Fix: `status()` now calls `_scan_sensors()` at the top of every cycle, so a bus that isn't ready at boot — **or a sensor that drops off and returns mid-run** (the OUT-sensor dropout case in the isolation note above) — recovers on its own within one daemon cycle, **no restart**. The scan is a cheap `/sys/bus/w1/devices/` directory read; a state change logs `OneWireTherm bus now has N sensor(s)` at WARNING; a scan that raises keeps the last-known list. Verified live: import-time empty list → 0 values one cycle → 4 values the next after the bus enumerated. **Consequence:** the manual-restart recipe below (and the "recovered only by reboot" import-time caveat in the isolation note) is now only needed for a bus so broken it never enumerates at all.

### OneWireTherm after physical repin/rewire — self-heals within one cycle now (was: restart the service; FIXED 2026-07-06)

previously, if `pivac-1wire` started while the DS18B20 pins were disconnected (e.g. boot during hardware work), it ran with an **empty sensor list and silently published nothing** — service `active`, zero errors, all 1-wire SK paths stale — and needed `sudo systemctl restart pivac-1wire` once the wires were back. With the per-cycle re-scan above, reconnecting the wires now recovers automatically within one daemon cycle once `/sys/bus/w1/devices/` shows the `28-*` sensors; a manual restart is no longer required (still harmless). Do **not** reboot for this.

## Cutover day on the DS2482 bus (2026-09-07)

The EXT board went into the panel proven only with the spare probe on H1. The field trunk showed nothing for the first half hour, for two reasons in sequence. First the H1 plug had VCC and GND reversed: the DS2482 reported line high, no short, no presence (status register after a 1-wire reset, read with the driver unbound), and neither the eight probes nor the board were damaged by several minutes of it. Second, the 22 AWG lever-connector pigtail that gets the 18 AWG trunk into the PTSM plug is an intermittent joint; with it seated all eight probes appeared at once, and the forty-sweep proof then passed clean (320/320 CRC, every sweep at 8, `ext_power` 1 on all). The spare probe on H1 was the test that split board from trunk: count 1 with clean reads meant everything on the Pi side was good. The CAT6 re-pull of `docs/ds18b20-bus-topology.md` §7.2 is the fix for the joint.
